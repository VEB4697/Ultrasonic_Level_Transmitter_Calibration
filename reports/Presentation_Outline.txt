SLIDE 3: Working Principle of Ultrasonic Level Measurement
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Time-of-Flight (ToF) Equation:
  Distance d = (v_sound * t) / 2
- Speed of Sound Temperature Compensation:
  v_sound = 331.3 + 0.606 * T (m/s)
- Liquid Level Formula in Vessel of Height H:
  Level L = H - d

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 4: Mathematical Modeling & Gaussian Elimination Curve Fitting
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Target Calibration Model:
  V_out = m * Level + c
- Normal Equations Matrix System [A][X] = [B]:
  [ sum(x_i^2)   sum(x_i) ] [ m ] = [ sum(x_i * y_i) ]
  [ sum(x_i)     N        ] [ c ]   [ sum(y_i)       ]
- Gaussian Elimination Steps:
  1. Construct Augmented Matrix [A|B].
  2. Perform Forward Elimination with Partial Pivoting to form Upper Triangular Matrix.
  3. Perform Back Substitution to compute optimal slope m and offset c.

SLIDE 10: Potential Examiner Viva Questions & Answers
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: Why use Gaussian elimination instead of pre-built functions?
A: Gaussian elimination allows explicit matrix solver control, step-by-step pivoting to prevent numerical instability, and embedded memory optimization suitable for microcontrollers.

Q2: What is the main source of error in ultrasonic level measurement?
A: Temperature variations changing the speed of sound (~0.17% error per deg C) and surface turbulence/foaming causing ultrasonic wave attenuation.

Q3: How does the 4-20mA current loop provide noise immunity?
A: Current signals are unaffected by wire resistance or voltage drops over long distance industrial cables, unlike voltage signals.
